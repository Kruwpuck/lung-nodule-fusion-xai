\begin{abstract}
A backbone comparison is usually read as a question about architecture, but three design axes move together in one: model capacity, the training protocol, and the architecture family itself. We ask which of the three governs performance and interpretability for lung nodule malignancy classification on LIDC-IDRI, and find that the decisive variable is none of them. It is the \emph{explanation site}: the layer at which a class-activation map is taken. Sweeping 86 candidate sites across twelve backbones, pointing accuracy against the radiologist mask moves by up to 0.633 within a single fixed network as the site is varied, which is larger than any between-backbone difference we measure at a fixed site. The canonical last-spatial-layer choice is best in only 4 of 12 backbones, and in none of the twelve is the deepest candidate site the best one. Automatic target-layer selectors fail silently on this task: of the 86 sites, 17 emit an identically-zero map on at least one sample and 12 do so on every sample, and because a constant-zero map still yields a finite Dice value (0.0350) and a finite pointing rate (0.0000), a selector that lands there reports a plausible number instead of an error. One published row of ours was produced this way: GoogLeNet scores 0.0000 pointing accuracy at the automatically resolved site, a $1\times1$ dropout layer with no spatial extent, and 0.7667 at a $3\times3$ site in the same network under the same weights. Capacity is a subordinate confirmation rather than a discovery: over seven backbones sharing a 96\,px input, a 9.7-fold parameter range (5.60 to 54.31\,M) spans only 0.0144 AUC (0.8911 to 0.9055), and the 27.82\,M model attains the highest AUC of the seven while the 54.31\,M model does not; the Spearman correlation between capacity and AUC is 0.50 at $n=7$ and is not significant ($p=0.25$), which at this sample size settles the capacity question in neither direction. On the protocol axis, a factorial ANOVA over four backbones attributes $\eta^2=0.4533$ of AUC variance to optimizer choice against $0.0002$ to weight decay. The practical consequence is that any cross-architecture explainability number reported without a target-layer audit is uninterpretable, and may be an artifact of the tooling rather than a property of the network.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A comparison of convolutional backbones on a medical imaging task is normally presented as an architecture study, but at least three variables move at once when the backbone is swapped. Capacity changes, because families differ by an order of magnitude in parameter count. The training protocol interacts with the architecture, because a single learning rate and optimizer cannot suit every family equally. And the architecture family itself changes, bringing a different stem, a different downsampling schedule, and a different classification head. If a comparison reports one accuracy number per backbone, none of the three can be separated from the others. This paper asks the umbrella question directly: among capacity, optimizer, and architecture family, which governs performance and which governs interpretability?

Our answer is that for interpretability the governing variable is not on that list. It is the layer at which the explanation is computed. We sweep 86 candidate explanation sites across twelve backbones and measure Layer-CAM localization against radiologist nodule masks at each. Within a single frozen network, pointing accuracy varies by as much as 0.633 across sites. That within-model range exceeds every between-model difference we observe at a fixed site, which reverses the usual reading of a cross-architecture explainability table: the table is largely reporting where the maps were taken, not which network produced them.

This matters beyond a methodological footnote because the site is usually chosen automatically. Explainability tooling resolves a target layer by a heuristic -- the last convolutional module, or the deepest module whose feature map falls in some size band -- and that heuristic can land on a module with no spatial extent at all. A $1\times1$ activation upsampled to the input produces a constant map. A constant map is not an error: it flows through the metric code and produces a finite Dice value and a finite pointing rate. The failure is therefore silent, and the number it produces is in the same range as a real one. We find 17 such sites among the 86, and one of them sits under a row we ourselves previously published.

The capacity axis behaves as the literature would predict, and we report it as a domain confirmation rather than a finding. On the seven backbones that share a 96\,px input, a 9.7-fold spread in parameters buys 0.0144 AUC, and the largest model is not the best one. The optimizer axis absorbs our earlier stability and label-granularity analyses: optimizer choice dominates weight decay in a variance decomposition by three orders of magnitude in $\eta^2$, so the protocol lever that matters is not the one usually swept.

The contribution is methodological and actionable. (1) We show that explanation site, not architecture family or capacity, is the dominant source of variation in cross-backbone CAM localization on this task, and we quantify it with a per-backbone depth sweep in which each curve is its own control. (2) We census the silent-failure mode of automatic target-layer selection, give its rate over 86 sites, and show it is silent by construction because degenerate maps still produce finite metrics. (3) We diagnose one affected backbone in full, with a layer sweep, a cross-method check, a cascading weight-randomisation test, and a class-flip probe, and report where that diagnosis stops short of a faithfulness claim. (4) We report the capacity and optimizer axes on the same cohort, with their subset memberships made explicit, so that the three claims are not silently read as resting on the same models.

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Capacity on small medical cohorts.} That additional parameters buy little accuracy on medical imaging datasets of this scale is established rather than novel. The canonical result attributes much of the apparent transfer benefit of large ImageNet architectures to over-parameterisation rather than to sophisticated feature reuse, and finds lightweight models competitive with them on medical tasks.\CITE{Raghu et al. 2019, Transfusion -- kapasitas tidak membeli akurasi pada data medis} A same-domain instance on a LIDC-IDRI cohort of about a thousand nodules reports the best AUROC from a simple CNN rather than from the deeper transfer-learned backbones.\CITE{Saied et al. 2023, Insights into Imaging 14:91 -- CNN sederhana mengungguli backbone dalam pada LIDC} Lightweight backbones on this dataset have been proposed with explicit mobile-deployment motivation,\CITE{Sahu et al. 2019, IEEE JBHI 23(3):960--968 -- CNN ringan multi-section untuk LIDC} and the efficiency framing itself -- parameters, FLOPs, latency, memory, and accuracy-per-parameter, with a Pareto plot -- is a settled convention,\CITE{Schwartz et al. 2020, Green AI, CACM 63(12) -- konvensi pelaporan efisiensi} \CITE{Strubell et al. 2019, ACL -- biaya energi pelatihan} with a radiology-specific version of the sustainability argument.\CITE{Radiology AI and sustainability paradox, Insights into Imaging 2025} We therefore present our capacity result as a confirmation on a new cohort, not as a discovery. We are also explicit that we could not locate a single peer-reviewed source reporting LIDC-IDRI classification AUC alongside parameter counts in one table; the precedent is assembled from partial sources.

\textbf{Cross-architecture CAM comparison.} The nearest precedent to our localization axis compares Grad-CAM overlap across VGG, ResNet, and ViT variants on pneumothorax chest radiographs and finds that map quality varies by architecture while diagnostic accuracy stays nearly flat, concluding that accuracy and explanation quality are not in step.\CITE{Qiu, Rivaz \& Xiao, MLMI@MICCAI 2023, LNCS 14349:224--233 -- preseden lintas-arsitektur terdekat} Our work extends that result rather than filling a gap it left open: we move from one modality to another, from custom overlap statistics to the pointing game and Dice, from a CNN-versus-ViT contrast to twelve backbones spanning several CNN families plus a ViT, and -- the substantive extension -- from a single fixed site per model to a sweep over every candidate site, which is what lets us attribute the variation to the site rather than to the family. To our knowledge, no prior work on lung nodule CT reports a per-backbone explanation-site sweep alongside parameter efficiency on a common cohort, but we state that as a hedged claim about our search, not as an absolute.

\textbf{Layer choice and the mechanism of failure.} The dependence of localization on layer depth is documented in the primary sources: the original Grad-CAM work observes that localization degrades toward earlier convolutional layers and that final-layer maps are coarse,\CITE{Selvaraju et al., Grad-CAM, ICCV 2017 / IJCV 2020 -- rekomendasi kanonik "layer konvolusi terakhir"; butuh nomor pasase, bukan sitasi selevel makalah} and Layer-CAM was designed precisely to recover fine localization from higher-resolution intermediate layers.\CITE{Jiang et al., LayerCAM, IEEE TIP 2021 30:5875--5888} What follows from those two facts, and what we did not find stated, is that the canonical last-layer recommendation is a default rather than an optimum, and that an automatic implementation of it can degrade into a site with no spatial extent while continuing to emit metrics. A mechanistic account of why maps go empty exists for large effective receptive fields and rectification effects,\CITE{Grad-CAM: The impact of large receptive fields and other caveats, CVIU 2025;258 -- peta kosong, receptive field besar} but it explains why a map can be uninformative, not that the selection of the layer fails without signalling. We are not aware of a source for the silent-selection claim itself, and mark it as such.

\textbf{Faithfulness is a separate question from localization.} Overlap with a mask measures where a map is bright, not whether the model used that region. Gradient-averaging CAMs are argued to be provably faithful only for heads of the gap-then-single-linear-layer form, which several of our backbones do not have,\CITE{Draelos \& Carin, HiResCAM, arXiv:2011.08891 -- teorema faithfulness untuk head GAP + satu FC} and the standard diagnostic is cascading parameter randomisation.\CITE{Adebayo et al., Sanity Checks for Saliency Maps, NeurIPS 2018, arXiv:1810.03292} We run that diagnostic and report, in Section~\ref{sec:limits}, that our maps only partly pass it. A gradient-free method provides an independent cross-check,\CITE{Wang et al., Score-CAM, CVPRW 2020} and the pointing metric we use originates in the excitation-backprop literature.\CITE{Zhang et al., Top-Down Neural Attention by Excitation Backprop, ECCV 2016 / IJCV 2018 -- asal metrik pointing game}

\textbf{Benchmarking with few models.} Twelve architectures and seven cost-profiled ones are too few to support a powered claim about families, and the benchmarking-variance literature argues for reporting variance and withholding strong rankings under such conditions,\CITE{Bouthillier et al., Accounting for Variance in ML Benchmarks, MLSys 2021, arXiv:2103.03098} as does evidence that a tuning budget can reverse architecture rankings outright.\CITE{"Tune It or Don't Use It" -- anggaran tuning membalik ranking arsitektur; judul/venue belum terverifikasi} We adopt that posture throughout. Comparative work in this domain more often varies modality, feature type, and architecture together in a single pipeline comparison \cite{prabhavalkarHybridPETCTRadiomics2026} than it isolates one design axis, which is the gap in method rather than in topic that we try to address.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}
The dataset, split, preprocessing, and label definitions below are stated in full. A companion paper on radiomics-CNN fusion uses the same cohort and the same fold assignment, and each paper must be readable without the other; restating the cohort here is deliberate and is not an independent contribution of either paper.

\subsection{Dataset}
We use LIDC-IDRI.\CITE{Armato et al. 2011, Medical Physics 38(2):915--931 -- basis data LIDC-IDRI} The cohort comprises 717 patients and 725 scans, with reconstructed slice thickness from 0.45 to 5.0\,mm (median 1.25\,mm). After per-nodule aggregation, 2138 nodule rows remain, with a median 3D maximum diameter of 11.0\,mm (range 2.45 to 54.28\,mm).

\subsection{Labels}
Each nodule carries malignancy ratings on the 1 to 5 scale from up to four readers; the nodule label is the median of those ratings.\CITE{konvensi agregasi label median pada LIDC} We define benign as a median below 3, malignant as a median above 3, and indeterminate as a median of exactly 3. Four label arms are trained. The \emph{binary} arm drops the 747 indeterminate nodules and retains 1391 (937 benign, 454 malignant). The \emph{ordinal} arm keeps the full 1 to 5 scale (260/677/747/353/101 nodules at ratings 1 through 5). The \emph{three-class} arm keeps all 2138 nodules with indeterminate as its own class. The \emph{four-class} arm adds 2138 hard negatives drawn from LUNA16 class-0 candidates as a no-nodule class, for 4276 rows. Unless stated otherwise, every AUC in this paper is a benign-versus-malignant AUC on the 1391-nodule binary-eligible set.

\subsection{Split and preprocessing}
The cohort is partitioned into five folds by patient identifier with seed 42, so no patient contributes to more than one fold, and every backbone and every arm uses the identical assignment. Each nodule is represented as a 2.5D patch: a stack of adjacent axial slices centred on the nodule, resampled and cropped to a fixed in-plane size. Hounsfield units are clipped to $[-1000, 400]$ and rescaled to $[0,1]$.

\subsection{Backbone set and the three subsets}
\label{sec:subsets}
The three axes of this paper do not rest on the same models, and the subsets are not strictly nested. We name them once and carry the names through the results.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{localization12} -- twelve backbones with Layer-CAM measurements: MobileNetV3-Small, EfficientNet-B0, ResNet50, VGG16 (64\,px input), DenseNet121, DenseNet201, GoogLeNet, InceptionV3, Inception-ResNet-v2, Xception, ConvNeXt-Tiny (96\,px), and ViT-Base (224\,px).\CITE{makalah arsitektur: GoogLeNet, InceptionV3, Inception-ResNet-v2, Xception, DenseNet, ConvNeXt}
\item \textbf{efficiency7} -- the seven of those twelve that run at a shared 96\,px input. Only these carry cost measurements, because GFLOPs, latency, and memory all scale with input size and mixing three input sizes in one cost column would invite exactly the comparison the column exists to support.
\item \textbf{optimizer4} -- MobileNetV2, EfficientNet-B0, ResNet50, VGG16. MobileNetV2 appears in no other subset, so optimizer4 is \emph{not} a subset of localization12.
\end{itemize}
Input resolution is treated here as an architectural variable rather than a nuisance parameter, because several of the twelve cannot accept a common input without upsampling that would itself alter the maps. Section~\ref{sec:limits} states what that costs us.

\subsection{Efficiency measurement}
For the seven backbones at 96\,px we report parameter count, GFLOPs per forward pass, median CPU latency with interquartile range, median GPU latency, and peak GPU memory. Latency is wall-clock and warm-started, at batch size 1, over 7 repeats of 50 forward passes each; the median over repeats is reported. Measurements were taken on an Intel Core CPU (family 6, model 151) and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3060 under PyTorch 2.3.0 with CUDA 12.1 on Windows. We also report accuracy per million parameters and a Pareto front over accuracy against GFLOPs. FLOPs is a poor proxy for wall-clock latency, and both are hardware- and batch-size-dependent, so the two cost columns are read together rather than either alone.

\subsection{Localization protocol}
Class-activation maps are computed with Layer-CAM\CITE{Jiang et al., LayerCAM, IEEE TIP 2021} on a fixed set of 60 fold-0 nodules with radiologist-annotated masks, identical across all twelve backbones. Maps are upsampled to the input grid, min-max normalised, and thresholded at the 80th percentile of map energy for the overlap metrics. We report Dice, IoU, a size-matched Dice variant that removes the dependence of raw Dice on the ratio between map area and lesion area, a pointing-game hit rate\CITE{Zhang et al., pointing game} recording whether the map's maximum falls inside the mask, and an energy-based pointing metric giving the fraction of map energy inside the mask.\CITE{energy-based pointing game, Score-CAM} Chance pointing accuracy is 0.0189, the mean lesion area fraction over the same 60 samples; a pointing rate near that value indicates a map carrying no localization information.

\subsection{Explanation site: the sweep and the two selectors}
\label{sec:sitesel}
For each backbone we enumerate every module that emits a spatial activation and order the candidates from shallow to deep by normalised execution index, giving 86 candidate sites over the twelve backbones (6 to 12 per backbone; 12 for ViT-Base, whose blocks all emit a $14\times14$ token grid). Layer-CAM is recomputed at each site with the same weights, the same 60 samples, and the same metrics, so a within-backbone curve of localization quality against depth is its own control: nothing but the site changes along it.

Against those curves we evaluate the two automatic rules we have used in practice. The \emph{canonical} rule takes the deepest module with spatial extent, the last-convolutional-layer default the CAM literature recommends.\CITE{Selvaraju et al., Grad-CAM -- pasase spesifik rekomendasi layer konvolusi terakhir} The \emph{band} rule takes the deepest module whose feature-map height falls in $[7,10]$, a heuristic intended to trade resolution against semantic depth. Both are resolved programmatically from the module graph, which is how a degenerate site can be selected without anyone noticing. We record, for every one of the 86 sites, how many of the 60 maps are identically zero, and whether the site is the canonical pick, the band pick, or the site behind our own previously published row.

\subsection{Diagnosis protocol for a suspected degenerate site}
A pointing accuracy of exactly zero is an extraordinary result and is treated as a suspected implementation artifact until excluded. For the affected backbone we run four checks. (i) A layer sweep, to establish whether any site in the same network under the same weights recovers localization above chance. (ii) A CAM-method cross-check at the recovered site, comparing Layer-CAM against HiResCAM, Grad-CAM, and the gradient-free Score-CAM,\CITE{Wang et al., Score-CAM, CVPRW 2020} \CITE{Draelos \& Carin, HiResCAM} to exclude a gradient-averaging artifact specific to that head. (iii) Cascading weight randomisation from the classifier backwards, reporting the Spearman rank correlation between the original map and the map from the progressively randomised model.\CITE{Adebayo et al., Sanity Checks for Saliency Maps} (iv) A class-flip probe, computing the map for the non-predicted class and its rank correlation with the original. A verdict of \emph{artifact} requires all three of: the resolved site emits an all-zero map on every sample; some candidate site recovers pointing accuracy above chance plus two standard errors with no zero maps; and the recovered map's mean rank correlation with the fully randomised map falls below 0.5.

\subsection{Optimizer and protocol axis}
On optimizer4 we train a full grid of 4 backbones $\times$ 3 optimizers (SGD with momentum 0.9, Adam, AdamW) $\times$ 3 weight decays (the PyTorch default and one order of magnitude either side) $\times$ 5 folds, 45 runs per backbone. We decompose AUC variance with a factorial ANOVA over backbone, optimizer, weight decay, and fold, reporting $\eta^2$ per factor. Stability is tested in variance rather than in point estimates: a Brown-Forsythe test (the median-based Levene variant, for robustness to skew) on AUC variance for each of the six backbone pairs, with Holm correction across that family.

The label scheme is a second protocol lever. Each of the four label arms is collapsed to a common benign-versus-malignant probability by renormalisation rather than by argmax, which preserves the continuous score AUC requires. The binary arm uses its malignant probability directly; the ordinal arm uses the cumulative probability of a rating above 3; the three-class arm uses $P(\text{malignant})/(P(\text{benign})+P(\text{malignant}))$; the four-class arm is first restricted to the nodule subset and then renormalised identically, which guards against the AUC inflation that a large pool of easy true negatives would produce. All arms are scored on the identical 1391 binary-eligible nodules per fold. Paired DeLong tests compare arm pairs on that common subset, valid here specifically because the arms share cases, and a Friedman test with Nemenyi post-hoc over per-fold collapsed-binary AUC gives an omnibus ranking that does not require the shared-cases assumption. For the ordinal arm we additionally report quadratic-weighted kappa, mean absolute error, and one-off accuracy on the native 1 to 5 scale.

\subsection{Statistical posture}
Twelve models and seven cost-profiled models are small samples. Correlations are reported as descriptive summaries with $n$ attached, never as inferential support for a mechanism. Pointing-accuracy intervals are Wilson score intervals at 95\% over 60 Bernoulli trials, chosen because several backbones score exactly zero and the normal approximation degenerates there; Dice intervals are percentile bootstrap intervals at 95\% over the 60 per-sample values with $B=10{,}000$ and seed 20260820. This study is observational and hypothesis-generating throughout.
